Urban expansion in sub-Saharan Africa is commonly measured through changes in built-up extent, while nationally comparable evidence on building height remains limited. We examine both dimensions across 296,668 built-up 500 m grid cells in Ghana using two 2018 Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) products: Average Net Building Height (ANBH), which estimates height over built pixels, and Average Gross Building Height (AGBH), which combines height with built coverage and therefore measures volumetric density. The two rasters are mutually consistent, but their distributions differ substantially. Within Ghana, 38.5% of pixels with a positive ANBH signal fall within the 2.50–2.55 m interval, and 14.6% of footprint cells have no GHSL height signal. An independent satellite product, WSF3D, shows 2.5–5.0 m of height variation among cells that ANBH places within a 1.8 cm interquartile range, and cell-level agreement between the two height estimates is weak (Spearman ρ = 0.18). A model of log(1 + ANBH) using only pre-2018 predictors explains 23.5% of the variance; built coverage in 2010 remains its strongest predictor under spatially clustered standard errors and a spatial error specification. A four-class classification of ANBH against pre-2018 horizontal expansion agrees with expansion more than expected by chance (κ = 0.28), but almost entirely because cells without a height signal fall in its lowest class; among cells with a signal, agreement is at chance level. National analyses of building height should distinguish net height from gross built density, and absent signal from low height, before drawing substantive conclusions.
